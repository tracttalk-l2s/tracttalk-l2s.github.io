The problem

Lip-to-speech (L2S) synthesis generates the speech of a talking face from silent video. It can restore the sound of archival or corrupted recordings, give a voice to people who have lost theirs, and support communication in loud places.

The task is ill-posed in a specific way. Speech is produced by coordinated movements of the articulators and by the excitation of the voice source, but a camera records only the lips and the jaw. The tongue and the larynx stay inside the head. Many sounds therefore look alike on the lips, and a single lip movement can correspond to several words.

Most existing systems map video into learned latent spaces in which the visible lips and the hidden tongue are entangled. When such a system mispronounces a word, nothing tells whether the lips were misread, the tongue was guessed wrong, or the decoder failed. An L2S system therefore has to read what is visible, infer what is hidden, and render the result as sound, and TractTalk keeps these three steps explicit.
